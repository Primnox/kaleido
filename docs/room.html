The room

One player. The room goes dark, the film is the only thing lit, and your cursor is a small light. VLC plays everything underneath. These are its screens.

01Coming backThe last thing you saw is waiting under the lamp. Everything else you've watched sits further back in the dark until your light reaches it.
You stopped here.
A little past the middle. The storm hadn't started yet.
02WatchingOnly the film. No bar ever sits over it. When your light drifts low, the film's own moments show up under your hand and nowhere else.
03Holding itPress and hold anywhere to stop. The house lights come up, the picture settles like a photograph, and the last few moments you just saw sit beside it.
The Long Way North, more than half behind you
04Pull and pushGrab the picture. Pull it and the moments before it slide in. Push it and you go on. Time is made of the film's own frames, not a line with numbers.
05RememberingYou don't remember timestamps, you remember what it looked like. The whole film opens as a blur of colour, and each patch comes into focus as your light passes over it.
06ReachingHold your hand still and the things you might want come to it. This is where VLC's buried powers live: sync, boost past the limit, colour, other voices.

07Near and farScroll and the picture comes closer and gets louder, or drifts back into the dark and gets quieter. The ring around your light says how much.
08The endWhen the credits start, the lights come up. The whole film lies there as one line of its colours, marked where you stopped along the way. The next one is already on the table.
You stopped three times. Once at the camp, once in the storm, once right before the end.
